# Lecture 4: Foundations of Cellular Neuroscience & Action Potentials

## 1. The Neuron and Resting Membrane Potential
The neuron is the fundamental excitable unit of the central nervous system.
Neurons maintain an electrical potential across their plasma membrane known as the resting membrane potential.
The resting membrane potential is defined as the electrical voltage difference across the neuronal membrane at rest, typically around -70 millivolts.
The sodium-potassium pump (Na+/K+-ATPase) maintains this ionic gradient by pumping three sodium ions out of the cell for every two potassium ions pumped in.
Potassium leak channels allow potassium ions to diffuse down their chemical concentration gradient out of the cell.

## 2. Generation of the Action Potential
An action potential is an all-or-none electrical impulse that propagates along the neuronal axon to transmit information.
Depolarization refers to a decrease in the absolute value of the cell's membrane potential, making the interior more positive.
When the membrane potential reaches the threshold potential (approximately -55 millivolts), voltage-gated sodium channels open rapidly.
Sodium influx causes the rapid rising phase of the action potential toward +30 millivolts.
Repolarization is the return of the membrane potential toward the resting state, driven by voltage-gated potassium channels opening and sodium channels inactivating.
Hyperpolarization denotes an increase in membrane negativity beyond the resting potential, caused by delayed potassium channel closure.

## 3. Synaptic Transmission and Neurotransmitters
A synapse is the specialized junction where a terminal of one neuron communicates with an adjacent target cell.
Neurotransmitters are endogenous chemical messengers that transmit signals across a chemical synapse from one neuron to another.
The influx of calcium ions into the presynaptic terminal triggers the exocytosis of synaptic vesicles containing neurotransmitters.
Glutamate is the primary excitatory neurotransmitter in the mammalian central nervous system.
GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is the primary inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain, inducing hyperpolarization by activating chloride channels.
Myelin sheath is an insulating lipid layer formed by oligodendrocytes in the CNS and Schwann cells in the PNS that increases the velocity of axonal action potential propagation via saltatory conduction.
