# Unit 2: Cellular Energetics and Molecular Genetics

## 1. Cellular Respiration and ATP Synthesis
Mitochondria are the double-membrane bound organelles responsible for aerobic ATP generation through cellular respiration.
ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the primary energy currency of the living cell.
Glycolysis is the metabolic pathway that converts glucose into pyruvate, yielding a net gain of two ATP molecules and two NADH molecules in the cytosol without requiring oxygen.
The citric acid cycle, also known as the Krebs cycle, occurs within the mitochondrial matrix and oxidizes acetyl-CoA into carbon dioxide, generating high-energy electron carriers (NADH and FADH2).
Oxidative phosphorylation is defined as the synthesis of ATP powered by the transfer of electrons from NADH or FADH2 to molecular oxygen through the electron transport chain.
ATP synthase is the transmembrane protein complex that synthesizes ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate using the electrochemical proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

## 2. DNA Replication and Gene Expression
Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is the double-stranded helical polymer that stores hereditary genetic instructions in all cellular organisms.
DNA polymerase is the enzyme that synthesizes new DNA strands complementary to a template strand in the 5' to 3' direction.
Transcription refers to the enzymatic process by which an RNA polymerase copies a specific DNA sequence into messenger RNA (mRNA).
Translation is the process where ribosomes decode mRNA codons into a specific sequence of amino acids to assemble a functional polypeptide chain.
Ribosomes are molecular ribonucleoprotein machines composed of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and proteins that catalyze peptide bond formation.
